# Privacy

Last updated 30 September 2026.

This policy covers **layr.dynshift.com**. The LAYR compiler, CLI and packages you install collect nothing and send nothing anywhere; this page is about the website.

## Who is responsible

LAYR is by **DynShift**, an independent software studio based in India, which decides what this website collects and why: the controller under the GDPR, and the Data Fiduciary under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023. Contact: [official@dynshift.com](mailto:official@dynshift.com), or the [contact and grievance page](/contact).

## What is collected, and why

| What | Why | Basis | Who handles it |
|---|---|---|---|
| IP address and browser details, in server logs | Delivering the pages and keeping the host secure | Legitimate interest | GitHub Pages, which hosts the site. DynShift does not receive these logs. |
| Your theme and privacy choice, in local storage | Remembering them | Strictly necessary | Your device only |
| Pages viewed, referrer, device type, approximate country, a random visitor identifier | Counting visits and seeing which docs help | Your consent | Google Analytics, on DynShift's behalf, **only when analytics is switched on for this site and you allow it** |
| Advertising identifiers in cookies, the page, approximate location | Showing, measuring and protecting advertising; personalising it where allowed | Your consent in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland; elsewhere legitimate interest, with the opt-outs below | Google, as an independent controller |
| Your email and message, if you write to us | Answering you | Legitimate interest | DynShift |

**The playground and editors run entirely in your browser.** Code you type is compiled on your device and never sent anywhere; a shared playground link carries the code in the URL itself.

## Analytics

When analytics is switched on for this site, Google Analytics 4 loads only after you allow it: in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland through Google's consent message, elsewhere through a two-button question from this site. Google Analytics does not store your IP address; it uses it to estimate your country and discards it. Google signals and detailed location and device data are off, and event data is kept for two months. See [how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites).

## Advertising

Advertising pays for the time that goes into LAYR, which is free and MIT licensed. It is supplied by **Google AdSense**. A few places carry one advertisement each, labelled *Advertisement*: a band on the home page, one slot in the Library, and the docs' side rail on wide screens. Never inside documentation text, code, API tables, the playground or any legal page. Google may set cookies or read similar identifiers to serve, measure and protect those advertisements, and, where you have allowed it, to personalise them, under [its own policy](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites). The cookies are listed on the [cookies page](/cookies).

## Your choices

- **Privacy settings**, in the footer of every page, shows what applies to you and lets you change it at any time. Withdrawing consent is as easy as giving it.
- **EEA, UK, Switzerland**: Google's consent message asks on your first visit, with "Consent", "Do not consent" and "Manage options". Nothing that needs consent is used before you answer.
- **US states with privacy laws**: use **Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information** in the footer (it appears where it applies).
- **Global Privacy Control**: if your browser sends it, this site treats it as an opt-out everywhere: no analytics and no personalised advertising.
- **Anywhere**: turn off personalised ads at [My Ad Center](https://myadcenter.google.com/), block cookies, or use an ad blocker. The site works the same; nothing here argues with it.

## How long it is kept

- Your privacy choice: up to 12 months in your browser, then you are asked again. Your theme: until you change or clear it.
- Analytics event data, when analytics is on: 2 months at Google; only totals remain after that.
- Advertising cookies: as listed on the [cookies page](/cookies).
- Emails: deleted within 12 months of the last message unless needed for a legal claim.

## Who else receives data

Google (advertising, and analytics when on) and GitHub (hosting). Both process data in the United States and elsewhere under their own safeguards for international transfers, including the EU Standard Contractual Clauses and the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Nobody else. npm package statistics shown in the Library are fetched when the site is built, not from your browser.

## Your rights

Wherever you are, you can ask what DynShift holds about you and have it corrected or deleted: write to [official@dynshift.com](mailto:official@dynshift.com), and you will have an answer within 30 days. In practice DynShift holds nothing about a visitor except emails they send; data held by Google's advertising is Google's to answer for, through My Ad Center and Google's own tools.

### India (DPDP Act 2023)

You may ask for a summary of your personal data and its processing, and for its correction, completion, updating or erasure; withdraw consent at any time, as easily as you gave it; nominate someone to exercise your rights if you die or become unable to; and have a grievance answered. Raise it first through the [contact and grievance page](/contact); if you are not satisfied, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India. This notice is available in English or any language in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution on request.

### EEA, UK and Switzerland

Access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, and withdrawal of consent at any time. You may complain to your data protection authority.

### United States, Brazil, Canada and elsewhere

DynShift does not sell personal data for money. Opt out of advertising "sharing" as described above. The same access, correction and deletion rights apply to you.

## Children

This site is not directed at children, and DynShift does not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18.
